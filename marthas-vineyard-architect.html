Martha's Vineyard

Our home territory.

Martha's Vineyard is one of our primary markets, and the island has shaped our practice more than almost anywhere else we work. Its landscapes — the glacial outwash ponds, the salt marsh edges, the scrub oak and pitch pine plains — demand a particular kind of humility from architecture. Buildings that do not step aside for those landscapes are not worth building.

Pond House — our Architectural Record House of the Month (November 2024) — is on the Vineyard. It sits above a wildlife sanctuary on helical pilings, with green roofs of native grass and untreated cedar siding that will weather to the color of the marsh.

Permitting

A complex landscape we know well.

Building on the Vineyard involves an unusually complex permitting landscape. Depending on location, projects may require review by the Martha's Vineyard Commission, local building departments (six separate towns), Conservation Commission, Board of Health, and — for historic structures — local historic districts. We know these bodies, their processes, and their timelines. We have never had a project denied at the permitting stage.

Materials for Island Conditions

Built for the coastal climate.

The Vineyard's coastal climate — salt air, humidity, freeze-thaw cycles — punishes synthetic materials and rewards traditional ones. Untreated Alaskan Yellow Cedar and white oak weather honestly; vinyl-binder paints and plastic trim deteriorate. We specify for longevity in these conditions as a matter of course. Questions

About working on Martha's Vineyard.

Do you work on Martha's Vineyard regularly?

Yes. Martha's Vineyard is one of our core markets. We have completed and have underway several projects on the island, including Pond House, which was named Architectural Record's House of the Month in November 2024.

How does permitting work on Martha's Vineyard?

Permitting on the Vineyard is more complex than most mainland Massachusetts communities. Depending on your property's location, you may need review by the Martha's Vineyard Commission, local Conservation Commission, Board of Health, and building department — each with its own timeline. We manage all of it.

Can you recommend contractors on Martha's Vineyard?

Yes. We have established relationships with experienced general contractors, structural engineers, and specialty subcontractors who work regularly on the island. Contractor relationships matter more on the Vineyard than almost anywhere else we work, because the island's remoteness makes logistics critical.

Can you work on a historic home in a Martha's Vineyard historic district?

Yes. We have experience with the island's historic district commissions, including in Edgartown, Vineyard Haven, and Oak Bluffs. We can assess whether your property is subject to historic review and guide you through the process.
