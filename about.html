The Practice

Dedicated to architecture that honors its landscape.

Beautiful buildings and landscapes are simply the ones we want to return to. Most of the time, the landscape is the thing our clients were drawn to, and it is our role to give them the most amazing views out, while impacting the exterior place as little as possible. However, sometimes a family compound or gathering point requires some scale and grandeur. Whatever the size, we try to make homes and businesses that are graceful, elegant, and light on the land.

Our Office

Lincoln Old Town Hall — 25 Lincoln Road, Lincoln, MA.

We work from the Lincoln Old Town Hall, a remarkable building completed in 1848 for $2,200. At its first Town Meeting that December, the community resolved it would be open without charge for "Political, Temperance, Antislavery & Peace Meetings." Lincoln's first high school operated on the ground floor beginning in 1852, and the town's inaugural public library opened here in the early 1870s.

When a new town hall was built in 1884, the original passed to private hands. In 1891 it was moved downhill and converted into a general store and post office. In 1918, owner Charles Sumner Smith moved the entire building to its present site at 25 Lincoln Road — a feat accomplished over several days while the store remained open for business.

In 1931, Henry Ford sought to acquire the building for his Greenfield Village museum in Michigan. Smith refused. Ford built a replica instead. The original remained in Lincoln, which is where it belongs. A Martin Luther King Jr. memorial service was held here in 1968. We are glad to be its tenants.

Our neighbor downstairs is The Little General — a curated shop carrying housewares, vintage finds, and specialty provisions. Worth a visit.
